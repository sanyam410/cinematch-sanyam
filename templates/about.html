ABOUT CINEMATCH

Recommend by story,
not popularity.

Netflix shows you what's trending. CineMatch shows you what feels like the film you already love.

How it works

Every film in our library — 7,500+ movies from IMDb, 1980–2020 — is converted into a 384-dimensional vector using a sentence-embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). The vector captures not just the plot, but the genre, director and lead cast of each film.

When you search, CineMatch embeds what you typed and finds the nearest vectors by cosine similarity — the math of "these stories feel alike." Director, cast and genre overlaps earn a small ranking bonus on top.

If your movie isn't in the library — say, a 1972 classic — we fetch its real plot and credits from the OMDb API, embed that instead, and recommend the library's closest semantic neighbours.

The honest limitations

  • The library has edges. Recommendations always come from our 1980–2020 catalogue. Search a film outside it and you'll get films like it — never the film itself, unless we have it.
  • The fallback is approximate. Out-of-library matches rely on OMDb's plot summary, which is sometimes a single sentence. Results are good, not perfect.
  • Meaning, not taste. CineMatch compares stories — it doesn't learn from users, track trends, or know what's popular this week.
  • Free hosting has trade-offs. On the lightweight deployment, movies outside the library may not be searchable; the full experience runs where the embedding model fits in memory.
  • Posters depend on OMDb. On its free tier, artwork and details can occasionally be missing or rate-limited.
